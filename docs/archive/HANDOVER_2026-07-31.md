## 3. Finding: LSQ automations do not fire on API writes

Kaustubh built an automation moving Company `Fresh` -> `Nurture` when Contact goes
`Fresh` -> `Engaged`. A bulk job then moved 17,011 leads `Fresh` -> `Engaged` via
`Lead/Bulk/UpdateV2`. Measured before and after:

```
10:34   Fresh -> Nurture backlog = 5,115
10:53   Fresh -> Nurture backlog = 5,111        (-4 in 19 minutes)
```

Four is ordinary rep clicking. An async queue under load would have collapsed toward zero. The
automation almost certainly triggers on **UI edits only**.

This is fine for its actual job - a rep changing a stage on screen tomorrow will work correctly.
But it has a real consequence for the rest of the design: **the planned call-outcome and
Opportunity-creation automations will likely behave the same way**, so anything driven by an
integration, the phone app, or a bulk job may silently not trigger them.

**Ask the LSQ SPOC directly: do automations fire on API-sourced changes, or only UI actions?**
The answer decides whether the reconcilers in `scripts/leadsquared/migration/` are temporary
scaffolding or permanent infrastructure.

## 4. Fresh now means "nobody has dialled this yet"

Decision by Kaustubh, 2026-07-31. Originally the three un-connected outcomes
(`Didn't Picked`, `RNR`, `Switched Off/Not Reachable`) mapped to `Fresh`, on the logic that no
human was ever reached. Operationally that broke the bucket reps hunt in: 17,019 leads they had
already dialled repeatedly were sitting in it.

`17-move-unconnected-to-engaged.ps1` moved **17,011 leads, 0 failures**. Fresh is now 4,521
genuinely un-dialled leads. The reason each one did not connect is preserved in Call Disposition.
`00-schema.ps1` was updated to match, so no future run reintroduces the old mapping.

**Accepted trade-off:** `Engaged` no longer means "reached a human" - roughly 79% of it is dial
attempts. Use Call Disposition to tell the two apart.

**Still unsolved:** reps read Fresh as *"assigned to me and I haven't called it yet"*. Because
leads get reshuffled between reps via bulk reassign, no static stage or field answers that - a
lead marked `Did Not Pick` by rep A and handed to rep B is genuinely fresh for B but still
carries A's disposition. There is no owner-assignment-date field on the Lead. The real fix is a
**reset on reassignment** (clear the disposition when Owner changes) or an owner-stamped field,
not a new stage value.

## 6. Dropdown vs stored value - the failure mode to remember

**Both instances were found by Kaustubh trying to filter and seeing nothing, not by any
verification step.** LeadSquared **stores a dropdown value that is not in the dropdown** rather
than rejecting it. The record reads correctly over the API; reps simply cannot select or filter
it. No write log reveals this.

- **Call Disposition** - the migration wrote plan names (`RNR (5+ dials)`,
  `Follow Up (pitch delivered)`, `Switched Off / Not Reachable`) that were not options. Two were
  also **dropped as unsupported by the data**: the legacy `RNR` value never recorded a dial
  count, and `Follow Up` does not establish a pitch was delivered. Decision: keep the six
  existing options, match the data to them. 7,570 leads normalised.
- **Disqualification Reason** - worse: 9 options, 12 stored values, **zero overlap, 61,919 leads
  unfilterable**. Reverting the data was impossible here because the old list cannot express
  `Not Interested - No Reason Stated` (25,527) or `Invalid Contact Data` (4,797) at all. Fixed by
  adding the 11 canonical L2 values to the dropdown.

Now clean: `16-verify-dropdown-coverage.ps1` reports *"every field is clean"* across all six Lead
dropdowns. **Run it after any future migration.** Checking one field at a time by hand is exactly
how the second mismatch survived after the first was fixed.

One cosmetic residue: `Call me Later` (1,495) reads with a lowercase "me". LSQ normalises a
written value to the dropdown option's own casing, so it cannot be corrected until the option
itself is renamed.
